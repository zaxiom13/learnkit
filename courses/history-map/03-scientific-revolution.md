| development | why it mattered |
|---|---|
| **Printing press** | ideas copied cheaply and exactly; enabled the Reformation and science |
| **Renaissance** | recovery of classical learning; perspective in art; humanism |
| **Reformation** | broke the Church's monopoly; wars of religion; later, toleration |
| **Age of exploration** | global trade, empires, the Columbian exchange — and colonisation, slavery and catastrophic epidemics in the Americas |
| **Scientific method** | Bacon (experiment), Descartes (reason), Galileo (maths + measurement), Royal Society (1660: "take nobody's word for it") |
| **Newton** | one set of laws for heaven and Earth; calculus (also Leibniz) |
| **Enlightenment** | reason, rights, liberty — Locke, Voltaire, Kant, Hume, Adam Smith |

```reflect
? What was genuinely new about the Scientific Revolution?
- mathematical laws + experiment, not authority
- public, replicable, communal knowledge (societies, journals, print)
- universal laws for heaven and earth
model: The new thing was a method and a culture: describe nature with mathematics, test it by experiment, and share results publicly so others can replicate them — rather than appeal to Aristotle or scripture. Newton's success, one set of laws for apples and planets, made that approach look unstoppable.
```
