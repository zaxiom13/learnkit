| thinker | idea |
|---|---|
| **Hume** | **problem of induction**: no logical guarantee the future resembles the past |
| **Popper** | science advances by **falsification** — a theory must risk being wrong; unfalsifiable = not science |
| **Kuhn** | **paradigms**: normal science → anomalies → crisis → revolution (Ptolemy → Copernicus; Newton → Einstein). Incommensurability |
| **Lakatos** | research programmes with a hard core and protective belt; progressive vs degenerating |
| **Feyerabend** | "anything goes" — no single method |
| **Quine–Duhem** | you never test a hypothesis alone, only with auxiliary assumptions |
| **Bayesian epistemology** | degrees of belief updated by evidence; the modern default in many fields |
| **Gettier (1963)** | justified true belief isn't enough for knowledge — one of the shortest famous papers ever |

**Scientific realism vs anti-realism**: are electrons real, or just useful fictions? The **no-miracles argument** (success would be a miracle if theories weren't roughly true) vs **pessimistic meta-induction** (most past theories turned out false).

```reflect
? Explain the difference between Popper's and Kuhn's views of science.
- Popper: normative, bold conjectures and refutations, logic of falsification
- Kuhn: descriptive/historical, paradigms, normal science resists anomalies until revolution
- tension between how science should vs does work
model: Popper says science should proceed by bold conjectures that we try hard to refute; a single clear failure should sink a theory. Kuhn, looking at history, says scientists mostly do "normal science" inside a paradigm and tolerate anomalies until they pile up and a revolution replaces the framework. Popper describes the ideal logic; Kuhn describes the social reality.
```
