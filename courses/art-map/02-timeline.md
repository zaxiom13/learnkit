| period | defining traits | names |
|---|---|---|
| **Classical Greece/Rome** | idealised bodies, contrapposto, proportion | Phidias, Polykleitos |
| **Gothic** | light, height, stained glass | Chartres, Notre-Dame |
| **Renaissance** | linear perspective (Brunelleschi), humanism, anatomy | Leonardo, Michelangelo, Raphael, Botticelli; Northern: van Eyck, Dürer |
| **Baroque** | drama, motion, light; Counter-Reformation | Caravaggio, Bernini, Rubens, Rembrandt, Velázquez, Vermeer |
| **Rococo** | pastel frivolity for aristocrats | Fragonard, Watteau |
| **Neoclassicism** | civic virtue, clarity | David, Canova |
| **Romanticism** | emotion, sublime nature, the individual | Turner, Friedrich, Goya, Delacroix |
| **Realism** | ordinary life, workers | Courbet, Millet |
| **Impressionism** | light, fleeting moments, outdoor painting, visible brushwork | Monet, Renoir, Degas, Morisot |
| **Post-Impressionism** | structure and emotion beyond Impressionism | Cézanne, Van Gogh, Gauguin, Seurat |

```reflect
? Explain how the invention of photography helped push painting away from realism.
- photography could record appearance cheaply and exactly
- painters turned to light, colour, emotion, structure, abstraction
- also influenced composition (cropping) — Degas
model: Once photography could capture appearances exactly, faithful representation stopped being painting's unique job. Artists turned toward what cameras couldn't do: the sensation of light (Impressionists), structure (Cézanne), emotion (Van Gogh) and eventually abstraction — while borrowing photography's cropped, off-centre compositions.
```
